# SAMPATI: business model and go-to-market research

Research date and retrieval date: **9 November 2026**. Geography: **India, beginning with flood response**. Evidence standard: official government sources, programme owners, first-party humanitarian organisations, and the local repository. No secondary market-size estimates are used.

## Recommendation

**[BM-R1 — recommendation] Start with an NGO implementation-and-support business, then test recurring managed hosting.** Seek a relief organisation that already moves physical aid, has a named operations owner, and can fund a small deployment directly or through an existing donor-approved project. Sell a bounded workflow: reviewed needs → protected stock → dispatch → confirmed arrivals/returns → remaining needs. The value hypothesis is less reconciliation work and a more inspectable delivery record. Neither benefit has yet been measured.

**[BM-R2 — recommendation] Treat B2G as a later procurement channel.** Build evidence through a drill and a supervised NGO pilot before proposing a government deployment. A district or state disaster authority may be a useful observer or coordination partner without being the initial contracting buyer. Public procurement access does not establish a funded requirement, contract award, or deployment readiness.

**[BM-R3 — recommendation] Do not charge affected households.** The payer is an institution; the intended beneficiaries are flood-affected people. Avoid per-beneficiary fees or relief-value commissions. A fixed deployment fee and scoped support contract make the buyer's spend easier to predict.

## Baseline and limits

**[BM-L1 — supplied project baseline]** The research brief describes a local prototype with dated flood-assessment review, human-confirmed needs, aid inventory controls, dispatch, partial/full arrivals and returns, net-arrival unmet-need follow-up, organisation roles/audit, and reviewed file ingestion. It reports no customers, revenue, pilots, managed hosting, or measured operational impact. This research did not independently retest those functions. Those absences are the brief's baseline, not a conclusion drawn from website searches.

**[BM-L2 — observed local source]** The repository's [LICENSE](../../LICENSE) is MIT and permits use, modification, distribution, sublicensing, and sale subject to its notice conditions. The report does not assume exclusive ownership of the workflow or a software-licensing moat.

## Offer, pricing and alternative revenue

**[BM-P1 — pricing hypotheses, not market prices]** Quote a clearly bounded paid pilot before offering a broad SaaS subscription. **Use the ₹60,000 eight-week pilot as the single pricing scenario on the deck**; the monthly amounts below are later experiments. These are experiment points in INR, excluding applicable taxes and third-party charges. Actual quotes must follow discovery and measured delivery cost.

| Offer | Test price | What the test assumes | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eight-week pilot | **₹60,000 total** | One organisation, one exercise or limited supervised workflow, import mapping, two training sessions, business-hours support, baseline/endline report and export. | No 24/7 promise, bespoke integrations or automatic operational decisions. Live use follows readiness checks. |
| Managed service, entry test | **₹15,000/month**, after scoped setup | A hosted instance, backups/restore checks, upgrades and up to six support hours/month. | Hosting capability is proposed; it is not currently offered or demonstrated. Extra support/change requests priced separately. |
| Managed service, higher-price test | **₹25,000/month**, same bounded core | Test whether higher responsiveness, named support and agreed reporting justify a higher price. | Do not imply that either price has been accepted by a buyer. |
| Customer-managed installation | Scoped implementation fee plus optional annual support | Buyer runs infrastructure; SAMPATI helps configure, migrate, train and maintain. | Price only after estimating install and support workload. Buyer retains MIT self-hosting choice. |

**[BM-R5 — recommendation]** Price by organisation/deployment and service obligation. Avoid charging per volunteer or field recipient. Use a short included-support allowance with a pre-agreed surge plan; relief incidents can create a workload spike that a cheap flat subscription cannot absorb. Maintain an export path and agree how support ends or transitions at contract expiry.

**[BM-R6 — alternative revenue hypotheses]** Paid onboarding and data-template mapping; annual maintenance for customer-managed deployments; train-the-trainer workshops and exercises; explicitly scoped integrations or report configuration; donor-sponsored deployments for smaller organisations; later deployment/support work through an established implementation partner. Keep grant/prize income separate from customer revenue. Avoid a donated-aid commission, sale of affected-person data, or unsupported “impact credits.”

## Unit-economic experiments and scenario math

**[BM-U1 — illustrative assumptions]** All numbers below are modelling assumptions, not supplier quotations, realised costs, forecasts, margins achieved, or valuation evidence. Labour is costed at **₹800/hour** to make founder work visible. Taxes, payment fees and any separately billed messaging or travel must be added where relevant.

| Scenario | Revenue | Assumed direct delivery cost | Contribution before shared costs | What it tests |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Eight-week pilot, bounded | ₹60,000 | 40 hours × ₹800 + 2 months infrastructure × ₹3,000 + ₹4,000 travel = **₹42,000** | **₹18,000 / 30%** | Can onboarding, support and evaluation fit in 40 hours? |
| Pilot, overrun | ₹60,000 | 70 hours × ₹800 + ₹6,000 infrastructure + ₹4,000 travel = **₹66,000** | **−₹6,000 / −10%** | Customisation can consume the pilot fee. |
| Entry managed service, normal month | ₹15,000 | 6 support hours × ₹800 + ₹3,000 infrastructure = **₹7,800** | **₹7,200 / 48%** | Is a small support allowance credible and sufficient? |
| Higher-price managed service, normal month | ₹25,000 | Same **₹7,800** | **₹17,200 / 68.8%** | Does the buyer value a supported service at this price? |
| Entry managed service, surge month | ₹15,000 | 30 support hours × ₹800 + ₹3,000 infrastructure = **₹27,000** | **−₹12,000 / −80%** | A flat fee needs a surge/support boundary. |

**[BM-U2 — portfolio arithmetic only]** Five organisations at ₹15,000/month produce ₹75,000 monthly contracted revenue and ₹36,000 contribution at the normal-month assumptions. Ten at ₹25,000 produce ₹2,50,000 revenue and ₹1,72,000 contribution. These are multiplication exercises, **not adoption forecasts or a TAM/SAM/SOM estimate**. Five clients require 30 included support hours/month; ten require 60 before onboarding or incidents. One founder's actual capacity must be measured.

**[BM-U3 — exclusions and cash test]** Contribution here excludes product development, security work, founder sales time, accounting, legal/contract review, insurance, office costs, capital costs and downtime risk. It is not net profit. At ₹15,000/month, a hypothetical ₹30,000 acquisition cost takes ₹30,000 ÷ ₹7,200 = **4.17 months** of normal contribution to recover, before shared costs or churn. No actual CAC, retention or LTV is known. A 50% pilot advance would provide ₹30,000 against the ₹42,000 assumed direct cost, leaving up to ₹12,000 to finance before the balance; actual cash timing requires a delivery schedule.

## Pilot design and validation gates

**[BM-V1 — proposed pilot]** Recruit **three prospective design-partner organisations** as a validation target, with consent before naming them publicly. Start the first deployment with one NGO; use the other two to test whether the same core workflow repeats. No design partners are currently claimed. Begin with a preparedness drill using synthetic or properly authorised historical data. One NGO, one warehouse, two receiving locations and a small named user group are test boundaries, not a scale claim. Rehearse a normal shipment, short arrival, partial arrival, return, stale assessment, duplicated import and unresolved need. Move to limited supervised live use only after the operator accepts the workflow, deployment controls and fallback procedure.

**[BM-V2 — measurement plan]** Capture the existing process before introducing SAMPATI. Compare matched exercise tasks using the same scenario and clear timing rules; log task order and operator familiarity. For a single real incident, report observations and confounders rather than claiming causal impact.

| Gate | Evidence to collect | Proposed decision rule — a test threshold, not a validated standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem and purchasing authority | At least ten structured interviews across programme operations, logistics, finance and CSR roles; see an authorised sample of actual workflow artefacts. | At least three organisations describe the same recurring gap and identify a budget owner; at least one agrees a paid pilot scope. |
| Usability and record quality | Observe users completing needs, stock, dispatch, arrival and return tasks; reconcile against the exercise ground truth. | Every scripted movement reconciles correctly; errors are recoverable; the operator signs off the ledger and export. |
| Workflow value | Staff minutes to reconcile and produce a status report; missing arrival records; unresolved discrepancies; training time. | Choose a minimum worthwhile improvement with the buyer before the test. Report the sample, baseline and result even if the target is missed. |
| Deployment readiness | Restore a backup, test authorisation boundaries, confirm data ownership/retention, demonstrate exports and a manual fallback. | No unresolved critical access or data-loss issue; named operational and support owners accept the deployment. |
| Commercial viability | Actual setup/support hours, invoice paid, budget source, procurement duration and renewal terms. | The buyer pays and signs an extension at an economically supportable scope; praise, registrations and a free-pilot letter do not count as revenue. |
| Repeatability | Repeat with a second independent organisation. | Similar core workflow works with template configuration rather than a new fork of the product. Otherwise reassess whether this is bespoke consulting. |

**[BM-R7 — interview prompts]** Ask who last reconciled a dispatch with a partial arrival, which tools and handoffs were involved, what could not be verified, who authorised corrections, how much staff time it took, what budget could buy support, and why the team would choose supported SAMPATI over its existing process. Ask for evidence and a bounded paid experiment; do not infer demand from agreement that disaster response is important.

## Partnerships and go-to-market sequence

**[BM-G1 — proposed first 90 days, not progress achieved]**

1. **Discovery:** approach an existing relief operator through a founder's real network or a public institutional partnership route. Use BM-F05–F08 as a starting research map. No outreach was sent in this research task.
2. **Design partner:** secure one named programme owner, a finance/procurement contact, authorised workflow samples and a written pilot scope. “Design partner” should not appear in a public deck until the organisation consents.
3. **Supported drill:** run the baseline and rehearsal, then produce a short joint findings document with what failed, support effort and the buyer's go/no-go decision.
4. **Paid limited deployment:** execute only the accepted scope and collect the actual cost/usage evidence. Do not claim an emergency-control role or a 24/7 service.
5. **Repeat and renew:** prove a second organisation can adopt the same core. Offer a support renewal only if the actual workload and willingness to pay justify it.

**[BM-G2 — partner roles]** Relief NGO: operational owner and training partner. CSR foundation: possible programme sponsor and reporting stakeholder. IAG/humanitarian network: discovery, coordination exercises and shared learning. University/incubator: founder support and technical review. Established IT/implementation provider: possible future installation/procurement partner. Logistics provider: authorised movement/status exchange after the core workflow is validated. These are proposed relationships, not current affiliations.

**[BM-G3 — B2G gate]** Before spending heavily on tenders, identify the actual procuring department, budget line, specification owner, deployment/security requirements, procurement method, support obligations and payment milestones. Review the relevant central or state rules and the actual bid. GeM registration alone is not government traction; a sandbox demonstration or informal meeting is not a purchase order. BM-F09 supports the route, not a claim of eligibility for every contract.

## Market sizing and defensibility

**[BM-M1 — evidence limit]** This report establishes no count of qualified Indian flood-response software buyers, no verified software spend, no contract pipeline, and no TAM/SAM/SOM. Total NGOs, Darpan entries, Sphere members, districts, overall CSR spend, relief budgets and people exposed to floods are different populations or budgets. Multiplying any of them by a proposed subscription would manufacture a market estimate.

**[BM-M2 — proposed bottom-up method]** Define a qualified organisation as one with recurring material movements, a named coordination/stock owner, an unresolved workflow problem, permission to deploy, and a reachable budget. Build a named account list and record why each qualifies, current tools, programme season, procurement route, budget evidence and interview date. Calculate only a prospect-list revenue scenario using validated prices; label it a reachable-account scenario, not a national market fact. Discovery counts are activity metrics until orders are paid.

**[BM-D1 — inference from MIT and the product baseline]** Source-code exclusivity is a weak moat: another team can host or adapt the MIT code. UI features, import templates and shipment screens can be copied. There is currently no proven proprietary dataset, network effect, switching cost, procurement advantage or distribution moat.

**[BM-D2 — defensibility hypotheses]** A durable service could earn trust through reliable deployment, restoration and incident support; deep knowledge of actual NGO reconciliation tasks; reusable integrations/configuration; training that survives staff turnover; and a documented record of accurate, inspectable operation. Each needs evidence. Multi-agency network value could emerge only with permission and active participation; customer or beneficiary data should not be treated as a captured asset. Open source can aid inspection and adoption while increasing competition for hosting/support.

## Slide-ready business model

**Working slide title: “NGO operations first; institutional support pays.”**

- **Buyer:** relief NGOs with recurring flood-response logistics; CSR programmes may sponsor a named NGO deployment.
- **User:** coordination and warehouse teams tracking confirmed needs, stock, dispatch and actual arrivals.
- **Offer:** implementation, training, managed hosting and scoped support around an open-source coordination workflow.
- **Pricing to test:** ₹60,000 for an eight-week bounded pilot; ₹15,000–₹25,000/month for a later managed service. These are hypotheses, with no paid validation yet.
- **Go-to-market:** recruit three prospective design partners → begin with one operator → measured drill/pilot → renewal and repeat with the others → later institutional procurement.
- **Evidence gate:** paid pilot, reconciled movements, measured staff workload and a signed support extension. No customers, revenue or measured impact are claimed.

**Suggested speaker note:** “We have a local prototype and a proposed service model. We need one relief operator to test whether this workflow saves enough reconciliation effort to support a paid deployment. We will validate support costs and renewal before projecting scale.”

## Research limitations

This is commercial research and a validation plan, not an eligibility ruling, procurement opinion, or financial forecast. CSR treatment and purchasing terms need confirmation from the actual sponsor and buyer. Two government PDFs/pages were accessible only through their official indexed excerpts; these limits are marked above. Search snippets from commercial aggregators and social media were not used as evidence. No organisations were contacted, no prices were surveyed, and no grant or procurement application was submitted.
