# SAMPATI: problem evidence and future scope

Research date and retrieval date: **9 November 2026**. Prepared for the pitch deck. This is a primary-source research note, not evidence of SAMPATI deployment or field impact.

## Recommended framing

**Proposed positioning:** SAMPATI helps civilian relief teams connect dated flood-impact evidence, reviewed local needs, stock records, shipment evidence, confirmed arrivals, returns and remaining needs in one accountable workflow.

The defensible problem is the work of maintaining this chain as information changes and organisations hand responsibility to one another. Do not imply that India lacks official warning systems or that responders lack coordination tools. Existing government and humanitarian systems already do important parts of this work. The proposed product hypothesis is that a usable, provenance-preserving workflow can make a particular partner's coordination task more reliable. That hypothesis requires partner interviews and a measured pilot.

**Keep these boundaries visible:** a forecast predicts a hazard; a satellite assessment observes conditions at a stated acquisition time; an impact estimate informs review; a local request states a reviewed need; a warehouse issue records goods leaving custody; an arrival record supports receipt at a named destination. None is interchangeable with another.

## A feasible staged roadmap — recommendations and hypotheses

These are **proposed priorities**, not commitments, achieved integrations or validated benefits. They complement the repository's locally implemented relief/inventory/delivery workflow. The current baseline was taken from `docs/ROADMAP.md` and `docs/DATA_SOURCES.md` on 9 November 2026; this research task did not rerun the code or independently verify their test claims.

| Priority / hypothesis | Deliverable | Completion gate and learning question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H01 — Start with a bounded civilian pilot | Select one willing local coordination partner, one defined response workflow and responsible roles. Host a limited pilot with HTTPS, scoped access, audit exports, backups, rehearsed restore, support ownership and a fallback procedure. First rehearse with fictional cases, then authorised real cases. | Partner accepts terminology, units, review/dispatch/receipt responsibilities and data retention. Demonstrate stock-to-delivery reconciliation and handover after staff change. Question: does this match actual work and reduce record-reconstruction effort? |
| H02 — Add offline capture with explicit reconciliation | Support queued evidence/request drafts, visible last-sync time and a paper-compatible reference. Preserve author/device/time/source and a local queue through interruption. Treat contested stock/receipt changes as reviewable conflicts; authoritative stock deductions remain guarded. | Rehearse disconnect/reconnect, retries, clock mismatch, duplicate entry, device loss and concurrent changes. Demonstrate no silent overwrite or duplicate issue and a documented master record. Question: can staff continue useful capture without losing accountability? Motivation: C06; architecture is a SAMPATI hypothesis. |
| H03 — Add only task-justified context sources | Begin with a link-out or permission-reviewed SACHET/IMD adapter when a pilot task needs it. Add NRSC/CWC context only through authorised, documented access. Allow partner reports, forms or assessment tools to provide evidence through the same validated boundary. | For each source: access/reuse conditions, schema, source identifier, observation/issue/retrieval times, geography, units, revision handling, stale/unavailable state, replay tests and a reviewed example. Test value with users before adding further feeds. No incoming feed converts itself into a confirmed relief quantity. |
| H04 — Make handover and partner exchange useful | Agree a minimal exchange format for request/item/unit/location IDs, decision status, evidence references, issues, accepted arrivals, undelivered returns and remaining needs. Export a dated handover package with owner and next action; keep partner imports attributable. | Two participants can reconstruct a case and reconcile a partial shipment without sharing a database. Test mismatched unit/catalogue identifiers and conflicting partner revisions. Question: can teams avoid retyping and preserve responsibility across shifts and agencies? |
| H05 — Evaluate a repeatable operational workflow | Run comparable tasks against the partner's existing method. Measure correctness, elapsed work, reconciliation and visibility of unmet needs using the definitions below. Include staff and affected-community feedback through a safe process. | Publish denominator, sample, baseline, exclusions, uncertainty, error cases and negative findings. Decide with the partner whether to expand, revise or stop. No improvement percentage is assumed. |
| H06 — Introduce measured decision assistance later | After sufficient clean records, trial explainable duplicate suggestions, missing/stale evidence prompts, stock-demand comparisons or proposed allocation options. Show inputs, reasons and uncertainty; retain accept/edit/reject decisions. | Validate against reviewed cases, compare with a simple baseline, examine subgroup errors and omission risks, and measure override/rejection. Assistance does not approve dispatch, certify a route, allocate scarce aid automatically or issue public safety instructions. Those remain with accountable authorised people. |

**Deck wording:** “Next: partner pilot and resilient capture. Then: permission-reviewed context and partner handover. Later: evaluated decision assistance.” Do not make offline sync or cloud hosting sound implemented unless separately verified.

## Define impact before claiming it

The metrics below are proposed evaluation definitions, not established sector benchmarks. Agree units, scope, start/end events and evidence requirements before a pilot. Compare similar task complexity and retain failures in the denominator.

| ID / proposed metric | Definition | Interpretation and guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M01 — Review correctness | Correct accept/reject/correct decisions divided by all adjudicated review cases, with separate omission and false-accept counts against an agreed reviewer reference. | Include stale, contradictory and incomplete evidence. A quick incorrect review is not improvement. |
| M02 — Time to reviewed need | Median and upper-tail elapsed time from a complete, eligible local request entering the queue to a recorded authorised review decision. Also record active staff minutes separately. | Report waiting and working time separately; do not silently start the clock only after difficult cases are resolved. |
| M03 — Stock reconciliation | For each item, unit and store, compare ledger balance with an independently recorded physical count; report number of mismatches and absolute quantity differences per item. | Do not sum kilograms, litres and kits into a meaningless combined quantity. A clean ledger alone is not a physical stock audit. |
| M04 — Handover evidence completeness | Proportion of sampled issues/arrivals/returns with required quantity, item/unit, date, responsible parties, reference and accessible supporting evidence under the partner's procedure. | Distinguish missing documents from falsified or disputed documents. A photo or signature alone is not conclusive proof of distribution to households. |
| M05 — Delivery reconciliation correctness | Proportion of sampled issue lines for which issued quantity equals accepted arrived quantity plus confirmed undelivered return plus explicitly unresolved quantity, all in one unit and after reviewed corrections. | A return to a store is not relief fulfilment. Damaged/lost quantities require their own attributable disposition; they must not disappear as a generic success state. |
| M06 — Remaining-needs accuracy | Agreement between displayed remaining quantity and an independent recomputation from current reviewed need less eligible, accepted arrival quantities linked to that need. Record over-fulfilment and correction cases separately. | Reservations, issues and goods still in transit do not reduce the delivered remainder. A need can be changed or cancelled through review; preserve history. |
| M07 — Timeliness and unresolved follow-up | Among eligible reviewed needs with a stated deadline, show amount accepted by that deadline per item/unit and age of outstanding quantities. Track time from a flagged outstanding need to a documented owner/next action. | “Delivered by deadline” requires arrival evidence. Changed deadlines and request cancellations remain visible to avoid improving the number by editing the denominator. |
| M08 — Field usefulness and equitable access | Task completion without assistance, staff effort, failed submissions, offline backlog age, complaints/referrals resolved through the agreed process, and safely collected feedback on usefulness/access. | Explain who was absent from the sample. Process metrics do not establish welfare outcomes. Reduced deprivation, mortality or economic loss would need a separate field evaluation and attribution design. |

Recommended pilot result sentence template: **“In [n] comparable tasks with [partner/context], [metric] changed from [baseline] to [observed result], with [errors/limitations].”** Leave the quantities blank until measured.

## Evidence-chain demonstration suggestion

Use a clearly fictional example: a dated flood observation prompts incident review; a local coordinator confirms a need of 100 identical kits; a custodian issues 80; a reviewer confirms 60 arrived and 20 were returned undelivered. The delivered remainder is **40 kits**, and the returned stock becomes available only through its recorded return. The original observation remains linked context, with its uncertainty and date.

The numerical example is an explanatory design scenario, not a field result. It makes the central proposition reviewable: preserve the distinction between reported impact, confirmed need, committed/issued resources, accepted receipt and what still needs attention.
